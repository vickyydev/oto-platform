# DESIGN ipad-box-network

## Summary
The decided shape holds: the till PWA is served from the cloud, boxes serve HTTPS on the LAN with public certificates, and every connection to the cloud is outbound. Four parts of the brief are unsafe or fragile as written.

1. **DNS credentials on boxes.** Per-box ACME DNS-01 puts a DNS API token on every Pi, including booth Pis in public mall space. A stolen box can then take over the otoplay.co zone. Issue certificates centrally in the cloud and push per-box certificates down. Boxes should hold no DNS credentials, and booth and kiosk-only boxes need no certificate at all, because Chromium loads from localhost.
2. **dnsmasq on two Pis as the park's resolvers.** This makes the boxes critical for all name resolution on the LAN, and iOS does not fail over between resolvers strictly in order. Put static host records and a rebind whitelist on the router (RutOS, OpenWrt or MikroTik class) and keep box dnsmasq only as a fallback.
3. **"The app must not care which."** That must apply to screens only, not to the transport. A till bound to a station must never fail over to the cloud silently for writes. The problems are receipt series, the till lease, unsynced box state and the absence of devices. Failover should be explicit, pinned per sale, and follow a ladder: retry, then re-home the station to a sibling box (works offline), then cloud fallback as a virtual station.
4. **A cold spare in a drawer.** It cannot obtain role, config or a certificate offline. Keep it powered as a warm standby.

**The central design move** is one station contract (`packages/contracts`) with one implementation, the box agent. "Cloud mode" is that same agent hosted in-process in the API as a virtual box per branch, with simulated or no devices. This gives several things at once:
- the remote-admin path;
- the cloud fallback;
- the Sprint 2 staging environment, where no hardware exists;
- a convergence test harness;
- a Transport abstraction that reduces to `{baseUrl, auth: bearer|cookie, wsUrl}`.

**Tokens.** Cookies cannot reach the box. The session cookie is host-only and opaque, and a Domain cookie would leak to boxes and to the marketing site. The box channel therefore uses an Ed25519 shift token sent as `Authorization: Bearer` with `credentials: 'omit'`. The box keeps an exact-origin CORS allowlist. WebSocket connections use a single-use ticket plus a manual Origin check.

**Surfaces.** The customer display, kiosk and booth are device surfaces served by the box itself and bundled with the agent release, so they have no version skew, no CORS, and cold-boot offline. They use one device-session mechanism. The till is the only surface exposed to version skew. That is handled by:
- a capability handshake at `/v1/hello`;
- CI contract tests against the previous agent release;
- a box-served break-glass copy of the till shell.

**Things I could not confirm from documentation.** None of the iOS or Render behaviour below was tested. Resolve it in the 2-3 day spike on a real iPad, Pi and router before building:
- whether Safari and installed web apps are subject to the iOS Local Network permission;
- the WebKit preflight cache cap;
- Wake Lock in standalone mode;
- WebSockets through Render rewrites.

## Key decisions
- **Keep the till, admin and launcher cloud-served on ONE origin (app.otoplay.co, path-mounted). Serve the display, kiosk and booth surfaces from the box, bundled with the agent release. Add a box-served break-glass copy of the till shell.** — Cloud origin gives SSO through the existing host-only cookie, a single install, remote admin, and the ability to leave a dead box. Device surfaces are bound to a box anyway, so box-serving them removes CORS, version skew and any dependence on the iOS service-worker cache for an offline cold boot. There is no UPS, and power can return before internet does. The break-glass shell covers both service-worker eviction and hard version incompatibility while offline. (rejected: (a) Box serves everything same-origin: a dead box takes the app origin with it, storage and install are per box, and SSO and the launcher break. (b) Separate subdomains per app with a Domain cookie: the cookie leaks to *.central boxes and to the marketing site, and needs CORS plus CSRF work. (c) An OIDC-style central auth origin: 10+ days of work for no benefit on a single origin.)
- **One station contract with one implementation: the box agent. Cloud mode is a virtual box (vbox) per branch, hosted in-process in the API. It has simulated or no devices, and its queue is applied to Postgres synchronously.** — The brief requires the same screens to run against the cloud, and Sprint 2 has no hardware. The cloud therefore has to implement the contract anyway. Hosting the agent avoids a second, Postgres-backed implementation of the money path. It gives remote admin, cloud fallback, staging and the convergence test harness together, and it exercises the sync path constantly. (rejected: A storage-port design with SQLite and Postgres adapters behind shared services. It doubles the test matrix on checkout, tax and wallet logic, and the two implementations will drift. A cloud that implements only lookups breaks "the app must not care which".)
- **Token transport to the box is an Ed25519 shift token in `Authorization: Bearer` with `credentials: 'omit'`. It is minted from the cookie session via POST /auth/station-token. Keep the opaque cookie for the cloud, and never set a Domain cookie.** — The cookie in `apps/api/src/plugins/session.ts` is host-only, httpOnly and opaque. It will not reach the box, and the box could not verify it offline. A parent-domain cookie would be sent to every box and would be same-site with the marketing site. (rejected: A Domain=.otoplay.co cookie. A box-set cookie for the cloud-origin till, which is a third-party-style cookie plus CORS credentials and is fragile under Safari. DPoP-style proof of possession now: over-engineering for this deployment. Keep a `cnf` claim reserved in the token format.)
- **The station contract on the box is RPC-style with static URLs (POST /v1/op/<name>, ids in the body). Long actions are async (202 plus jobId, progress on the channel).** — Bearer tokens and JSON force a CORS preflight, and preflight caches are keyed per URL with browser-capped lifetimes. Static URLs keep about 50 cache entries warm. iOS suspends backgrounded pages, so a 60 s EDC sale held on one HTTP request would be lost. (rejected: REST paths with ids, which cost one preflight per entity. Synchronous long-held payment requests.)
- **WebSocket channel per station with single-use ticket auth, a manual Origin check, snapshot-plus-rev semantics, a 10 s app-level ping, and a polling fallback.** — Browsers cannot set headers on WebSocket or EventSource. CORS does not protect WebSockets. iOS drops sockets on lock or background, so correctness cannot depend on message continuity. (rejected: SSE, which is one-way, while the display must send intents. Diff or event streams requiring gapless delivery. A token in the WebSocket URL, which leaks into logs.)
- **Transport is chosen by station binding. Leaving the box is explicit and pinned per sale. The ladder is: retry, re-home to a sibling box, vbox fallback (virtual station), paper.** — Silent failover splits receipt series, bypasses the till lease, hides unsynced box state such as a member created 30 s ago or a wallet spend, and leaves the till with no devices. Re-homing uses branch config every box already caches, and Ethernet printers that any box can drive. It does not violate "boxes do not talk to each other". (rejected: Automatic silent cloud failover. The cloud driving LAN printers through a sibling box as the normal path; the brief explicitly excludes cloud-in-the-loop device actions.)
- **One active till per station is enforced with a box-issued lease plus a fencing epoch carried on every mutating op. The lease is per device. Take-over is audited and needs a stale holder or a permission.** — An advisory lock without fencing lets a stale iPad resume after an iOS suspension and write into a sale another iPad has taken over. (rejected: Cloud-side locking, which does not work offline. A per-staff lease, which breaks shift handover on one iPad.)
- **Customer display: the box owns a persisted StationSession document. The till sends ops and the display sends intents. The box recomputes totals, broadcasts full snapshots, and sends the display a redacted view. The display pairs by code into a device session. The split-screen harness is kept as a loopback degraded mode.** — This replaces prop-drilling across 6 host surfaces and the D8 pending-lookup hack. Full snapshots are idempotent under dropped sockets. Persistence survives a watchdog reboot mid-sale. One device-session mechanism serves the display, kiosk, booth and the reception kiosk (SCRUM-116). (rejected: Till-to-display peer messaging. A cloud-relayed display, which needs internet. A display that computes totals locally; it currently calls computeTotals and getTaxConfig itself.)
- **Certificates are issued centrally by the cloud (ACME DNS-01), per box, and pushed over the box channel. Boxes hold no DNS credentials. Localhost-only boxes (booth, kiosk-only) get no certificate. `cert_not_after` is reported in the heartbeat. The spare is kept as a warm standby.** — A DNS API token on a Pi in a public mall booth is a route to zone takeover. A spare that has sat cold in a drawer will have an expired or missing certificate and no role or config exactly when it is needed. Certificate lifetimes are shrinking, to 45-47 days by 2028-29. (rejected: Per-box Caddy DNS-01 as decided. It becomes acceptable only with a delegated sub-zone or an acme-dns CNAME plus a scoped token. A branch wildcard shared by all boxes: one stolen key lets an attacker on the LAN intercept traffic to every other box in the branch. A private CA installed by MDM, which breaks unmanaged admin devices.)
- **Local DNS lives on the router as static host records with a rebind whitelist. Box dnsmasq is the fallback only. Block the Private Relay canary domains. Use supervised iPads with no encrypted-DNS profiles. Use an uncommon subnet per branch.** — This keeps the Pis out of the path of all LAN name resolution. iOS treats resolvers as equivalent rather than as an ordered failover list. Router DNS proxies commonly strip private-IP answers unless the domain is whitelisted. (rejected: dnsmasq on two boxes as the primary resolvers. Relying on public A records alone, which fails offline and under rebind filters. mDNS or IP literals, which cannot carry a publicly trusted certificate.)
- **Capability negotiation through /v1/hello (contract major/minor, minClientContract, capabilities[]). The UI gates on capabilities. CI tests the current PWA against the previous agent release. Deploy order is cloud, then agents in waves, then gated PWA features.** — The PWA updates instantly on deploy. Boxes update in waves and can be offline for days, so skew is the normal state rather than an edge case. (rejected: Lock-step deploys, which are impossible with offline boxes. Version-number comparisons scattered through the UI.)
- **The PWA must be installed to the Home Screen. Service-worker updates are prompt-only and applied at the lock screen. All chunks and the barcode WASM are precached. Durable state is never kept on the iPad.** — A Safari tab is subject to the ITP 7-day storage cap. An auto-reload mid-sale is unacceptable. A redeploy on a static host removes old lazy chunks. iOS can still evict storage from an installed app. (rejected: An IndexedDB outbound queue on the iPad. `autoUpdate` service-worker mode. Lazy-loaded route chunks fetched from the network at runtime.)

## Risks
- [critical] A DNS provider API token on every box (Caddy DNS-01 as decided). A stolen booth or counter Pi allows takeover of the otoplay.co zone: certificates for app.otoplay.co, credential phishing, MX hijack. — MITIGATION: Issue centrally in the cloud and push per-box certificates. At minimum, delegate central.otoplay.co as its own zone, or CNAME _acme-challenge to a throwaway zone with a record-scoped token. Encrypt the data partition and make the device key revocable from the admin console.
- [critical] Silent failover of a bound till to the cloud. It causes duplicate or divergent sales and receipt-number collisions. It bypasses the till lease. Reads miss unsynced box state, and no devices are available. — MITIGATION: Drive transport choice from the station binding. Pin the transport per sale. Require a staff-confirmed switch. Give cloud fallback its own virtual station and receipt series. Re-probe and return to the box automatically between sales.
- [critical] The box dies, or the iPad disconnects, while an EDC payment is in flight. The outcome is unknown and the terminal is tethered to the dead box. — MITIGATION: The payment state machine lives on the box and is persisted per step. The iPad re-reads payment.state from the snapshot on reconnect. If the box is dead, use the audited manual-confirm path against the terminal's screen or slip before re-homing. Never retry a sale blindly. Inquiry is mandatory wherever the adapter supports it.
- [critical] iPads cannot resolve or reach the box when the internet is down. Causes: router rebind protection stripping private A answers, Private Relay or encrypted DNS bypassing local DNS, the iPad hopping to a known mall Wi-Fi network, or AP/client isolation. — MITIGATION: Static records on the router with a rebind whitelist. NXDOMAIN for mask.icloud.com and mask-h2.icloud.com. Supervised iPads with no iCloud, no DNS profiles, auto-join the park SSID only, and Wi-Fi Assist off. Boxes wired. Put an "unplug the WAN" test on the router in the bring-up checklist and the quarterly drill.
- [high] The PWA is used in a Safari tab, or its storage is evicted. An offline cold start then shows a blank page, and a standalone app has no reload button. The station binding and token are lost. — MITIGATION: Enforce installed mode. Call storage.persist(). Add a service-worker navigation fallback with auto-retry. Put a QR sticker at each counter pointing to the box-served /till shell. Make the binding re-pickable offline from any reachable box.
- [high] Version skew: a new PWA against an old agent (a box offline for days, or wave rollout), or a stale PWA against a new agent. — MITIGATION: The /v1/hello capability handshake. An additive-only contract with expand/contract migrations. CI contract tests against the previous agent release. A fleet skew dashboard fed by heartbeats. A blocking screen offering one-tap OTA, or the box-served shell when offline.
- [high] Certificate expiry on a cold spare or a box that has been off for a long time. CA lifetimes are shrinking: Let's Encrypt has announced 45-day certificates by about 2028, and the CA/B Forum 47 days by 2029. Safari fetch has no click-through on an expired certificate. — MITIGATION: Keep the standby warm and registered. Renew centrally at 50 percent of lifetime. Report cert_not_after in the heartbeat and alert below 21 days. Add a monthly power-on of any cold unit to the runbook. Let's Encrypt has dropped OCSP, so handshakes make no OCSP fetch; never enable must-staple.
- [high] iOS suspends JavaScript, timers and sockets on lock or background. Missed channel messages follow. The inactivity timer in OperatorContext uses setTimeout and drifts. Long HTTP requests are killed. — MITIGATION: Snapshot-plus-rev protocol. A visibilitychange handler that re-probes, fetches a new ticket, reconnects and pulls the snapshot. A wall-clock inactivity check. Async commands. Auto-Lock set to Never and Guided Access on till and display iPads.
- [high] The bearer shift token is readable by JavaScript in IndexedDB (XSS). It is also sent to whatever boxHost the station record names. — MITIGATION: Strict CSP with no third-party scripts (remove the Google Fonts CDN from `apps/pos/index.html`). Shift-length expiry, aud=branch, and a revocation list pushed to boxes. Client-side suffix allow-list for boxHost. A reserved `cnf` claim for later key binding.
- [high] Same-site CSRF. The Replit-built marketing site shares the otoplay.co site with app.otoplay.co, so SameSite=Lax does not stop its requests. Body-less POSTs such as sign-out and pending-lookup consume are "simple" requests. — MITIGATION: A global onRequest hook in apps/api that rejects mutating requests whose Origin is not the app origin. Require application/json on mutations. Consider hosting the website on a different registrable domain.
- [high] Out-of-scope navigation in an installed iOS web app opens an overlay browser. A root-level /login page or launcher redirect would break sign-in inside the POS. — MITIGATION: Set manifest scope to '/' with start_url '/pos/'. Render sign-in from a shared auth package inside each app. Register each app's service worker at its own path scope. Verify on real iPadOS versions in the spike.
- [medium] The in-process vbox adds load and a second storage engine to a 1 CPU API. better-sqlite3 is synchronous, and the cache must be rebuilt on restart. — MITIGATION: Apply the queue synchronously through the same ingest service, so there is no durability gap. Use a rebuildable cache: a Render disk, or :memory: with a change-feed rebuild. Keep operations small. Measure, and move the vbox to a separate Render service behind the same origin if needed.
- [high] Re-homing a station while the dead box holds unsynced sales. Receipt numbering continuity is at risk, and there is double-adoption risk while offline. — MITIGATION: Key the receipt series by (station, box) or by box, and confirm that with the accountant. Use an adoption epoch in the fencing token. Reconcile through the cloud on reconnect. If the board or PSU failed, the dead box's NVMe can be moved into the spare Pi.
- [medium] The Local Network permission and LNA prompts. iOS behaviour differs between Safari and installed web apps, and I have not confirmed it. Chrome 142+ has an LNA prompt and macOS 15 has an app permission, which affect admin laptops. A denial looks like "box down". — MITIGATION: Test in the spike. Add a diagnostic hint to the unreachable screen. Set a Chromium policy on the Pis. Remote admins are unbound, so they never probe the LAN.
- [medium] Camera scanning. Safari has no BarcodeDetector. Standalone apps re-prompt for the camera. A WASM decoder must work offline. A Bluetooth HID scanner hides the on-screen keyboard. — MITIGATION: Precache a zxing-wasm decoder. Make the box-attached scanner primary, with scan events routed over the channel. Use in-app keypads. Document the scanner's keyboard-toggle.
- [medium] Chromium kiosk robustness on a Pi 5 with 4 GB: crash pages, focus loss for the HID button, screen blanking, a read-only root versus the browser profile, 4K TVs, and no OS keyboard. — MITIGATION: A localhost origin. A cage or labwc kiosk with the listed flags. Profile on tmpfs or the data partition. A page-to-agent heartbeat that restarts Chromium. Force 1080p. An in-app keypad. A nightly reboot.
- [medium] The box clock is wrong after a power loss, because there is no UPS and the RTC battery is unfitted. Token exp checks break and occurred-at stamps are wrong. — MITIGATION: Fit RTC batteries. The agent refuses future-dated tokens only beyond a tolerance, and persists the last-known-good time. /v1/hello returns the box time and the PWA flags skew above 2 minutes. The cloud records clock_offset at sync.
- [medium] Customer display PII exposure, and the display iPad being used to reach staff functions. — MITIGATION: A redacted displayView projection. A device session with role=display that can call only display intents. Pairing is revocable from the admin console. Guided Access on the display.
- [medium] CORS and WebSocket misconfiguration on the box: a wildcard origin, a missing Vary header, no Origin check on WebSocket upgrade, or Host-header DNS rebinding against the box. — MITIGATION: An exact origin allow-list from cloud config. Shared contract tests that assert the CORS headers. Origin and Host validation on upgrade. Rate-limit unlock and PIN endpoints.
- [medium] Hosting topology. Static sites plus rewrites may not carry WebSockets. Old lazy chunks 404 after a deploy. No prod config exists today (no Dockerfile or render.yaml, trustProxy unset, COOKIE_SECURE false). — MITIGATION: One Fastify service serving /api, /api/ws and the static bundles. Precache all chunks. Set trustProxy and COOKIE_SECURE=true. Register routes under /api/v1.
- [medium] Booth boxes on unknown mall connectivity (captive-portal Wi-Fi) cannot reach the cloud, and the voucher printer may be unreachable. — MITIGATION: A small 4G router per booth or a wired mall port. A USB or booth-LAN printer. Booths need only outbound internet and localhost.
- [low] DHCP reservations are MAC-bound, so a board swap gets a new IP and DNS points at nothing. HTTP/3 Alt-Svc caching can also misbehave on private IPs after network changes. — MITIGATION: An agent expected-IP self-check that adds an alias and raises an alert. Restrict Caddy protocols to h1 and h2 on boxes.

## Disagreements with brief
- Section 6, "Boxes obtain Let's Encrypt certificates via DNS-01 with Caddy and the DNS provider module": unsafe as written. It places a DNS API credential on every Pi, including booth units in public mall space, and a stolen box can take over the otoplay.co zone. Better: the cloud performs DNS-01 and pushes per-box certificates. Boxes hold no DNS credentials. Booth and kiosk-only boxes need no certificate, because Chromium uses localhost. If per-box ACME is kept, require a delegated sub-zone or an acme-dns CNAME with a record-scoped token.
- Section 6, "Certificates are valid 90 days, so an internet outage never invalidates them": true only for running boxes and outages shorter than about 30 days. It is false for the cold spare, and it is weakening as CA lifetimes shrink (Let's Encrypt has announced 45 days by about 2028; CA/B Forum 47 days by 2029). Add cert_not_after to the heartbeat, alert on it, and renew centrally at 50 percent of lifetime.
- Section 6, "dnsmasq on two boxes ... The router hands out both as DNS servers": this puts the Pis in the path of all LAN name resolution, including reaching the cloud. iOS does not treat multiple resolvers as an ordered failover list. Prefer static host records plus a rebind whitelist on the router, which is already the LAN's single point of failure, and specify a router class that supports it. Keep box dnsmasq only as the fallback. If it is kept, both boxes must hold identical records and the router must never be a third resolver. Either way, block the Private Relay canary domains and forbid encrypted-DNS profiles.
- Section 5, "the same screens go via the cloud instead. The app must not care which": that is correct for screens and unsafe for the transport layer. A bound till must never switch to the cloud silently. The reasons are receipt series, the till lease, unsynced box state and the absence of devices. The switch should be explicit, pinned per sale and shown to staff. Cloud mode should be the same agent hosted as a virtual box, not a second implementation.
- Section 4, "A pre-imaged spare per park" kept cold: it cannot get its role, config or certificate offline, and its cache will be stale. Keep it powered as a registered warm standby, and support re-homing a station to a sibling box offline.
- Section 5, "POS in Safari, PWA": it must be the installed Home Screen web app, enforced by the app. A Safari tab is subject to the 7-day storage cap, has separate storage, and gives no standalone behaviour.
- Section 5, "Offline unlock verifies the existing token": a token proves session continuity, not knowledge of a password. Unlocking with a secret needs a verifier on the box. Use a separate short PIN (argon2id, rate-limited) rather than replicating password hashes to Pis in a mall. Also, the Sprint 1 inactivity lock destroys the session, which contradicts this item.
- Section 6, "fixed addresses via DHCP reservation" alone: reservations are MAC-bound and break on a board swap. Add an agent-side expected-IP self-check that adds an alias and raises an alert. Address boxes by box name, not by station name as in the brief's fd1 example, because stations can be re-homed.
- Section 7.2, rendering 2C2P's hosted QR image URL directly on the customer display: have the box fetch and serve it. The display then depends only on the box, its CSP can stay at img-src 'self', and the automatic switch to PAX QR lives in one place.
- Section 8, per-station receipt prefix: make the series key (station, box) or per box. A station adopted by a sibling box must not collide with the dead box's unsynced numbers, and cloud-fallback sales need their own series. Take this to the accountant along with the prefix question.

## Open questions
- Router: make and model, admin access, and whether it supports static DNS host records plus a rebind-protection whitelist. If not, will the park buy a router in the Teltonika RUTX, MikroTik or Peplink class? Are SIM plans capped? This affects OTA bundle size and sync.
- Are the till and display iPads park-owned, and can they be supervised (Apple Business Manager plus an MDM)? Are they Wi-Fi-only or cellular? Which models and iPadOS versions? Older iPadOS lacks storage.persist, Wake Lock and the web-app fixes.
- Who controls DNS for otoplay.co, and which provider hosts it? Can central.otoplay.co (and the branch-2 zone) be delegated as a separate zone? Will the staff suite live at app.otoplay.co, separate from the marketing site?
- Is there a guest Wi-Fi on the same router? Can staff, boxes and printers sit on their own SSID/VLAN with client isolation off for that segment only?
- When an admin works from home, do they need to complete sales (phone orders, invoices paid by 2C2P link), or only to view and manage? Default: remote sales are allowed only for payment methods that need no device, with digital receipts.
- When a counter's box dies, what is the acceptable degraded mode: move customers to the other counter, re-home the station to the sibling box (Ethernet printers keep working, the card terminal runs standalone), or cloud fallback with no printing?
- Can the spare box stay powered and online as a warm standby rather than sitting in a drawer?
- Accountant: for abbreviated tax invoices, is the numbering series per station or per machine (the box)? May a re-homed station and cloud-fallback sales use their own series prefixes?
